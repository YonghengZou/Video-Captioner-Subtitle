HOOK (0-5s)
What if an AI could read a neural network’s brain, and then rewrite it, without any training at all?

SETUP (5-20s)
Researchers at MIT just published something they call Weight-Space Programming. Normally, to change a neural network, you train it with gradient descent. These researchers skipped that. They gave a frontier AI the raw numbers inside a network, and just asked it questions.

THE PROOF (20-55s)
First, reading. Handed about seventeen thousand raw weights, the AI correctly named which game the network was trained on. Breakout, Pong, Pac-Man, Freeway. All four, right.

Then, writing. With no training, no data, and no practice runs, it wrote a network from scratch that plays Breakout. It beat a network trained for a hundred million steps.

And the wildest part: they gave it a small language model that kept getting stuck in thinking loops. The AI found the problem inside the weights and edited just point-zero-one-six percent of them. Math accuracy jumped from eight percent to forty-eight percent.

HONEST CAVEAT (55-65s)
It’s early. On another model that had no loop problem, the AI couldn’t improve anything. Editing weights can remove an obstacle, but it can’t add an ability the model lacks.

CLOSE (65-75s)
But imagine auditing an AI for hidden backdoors just by reading its weights. We may be watching the beginning of a new way to program AI. Follow me for more, and tell me in the comments: would you trust an AI to edit another AI?
